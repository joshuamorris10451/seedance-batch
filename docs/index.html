What Seedance 2.0 actually is

Launched in China 12 February 2026. Native synchronised audio — dialogue, foley and ambience generated in the same pass as the picture, which Veo and Kling still largely bolt on. Up to 15 seconds, multi-shot with real cuts inside a single generation, and it accepts up to 9 images, 3 video clips and 3 audio clips as references at once.

It beats what we're already using

On the Artificial Analysis leaderboards it sits #2 for both text-to-video and image-to-video — ahead of Veo 3.1 and Kling 3.0, behind only Google's Gemini Omni Flash. Our veo-flow-multi rig drives Veo 3.1. This is a straight upgrade on quality, and unlike Flow it has an actual API.

Now price acquiring it. You are renting infrastructure indefinitely to chase a one-time $14 — and the same $14 buys 18 clips outright, with no watermark, at full resolution, in about a minute.

00:24 · the tools you saw

What's actually being sold

I went looking for the tools. They sort into three piles, and only one of them does anything.

  • Multi-account managers — real, and useless for this. Projects like jimeng-api wrap Dreamina's web session into an API and rotate between tokens. They work. But every one of them requires you to already own the accounts and burns your credits. They create nothing.
  • Account creators — real code, doesn't survive contact. The public ones are ~20-star weekend projects with no CAPTCHA solving, no proxy rotation, and public temp-mail addresses. Their own READMEs admit the site changes and breaks them.
  • Scams and malware — the biggest pile by volume. Check Point documented a campaign of ~70 sponsored Facebook ads driving to convincing fake Kling AI sites; the "generated video" download was a RAT that watched 50+ crypto wallet extensions. Attacks using malware disguised as AI tools ran 33,000+ in the first four months of 2026, roughly five times all of 2025.
  • Fake Seedance resellers are a named category. Three flavours: reverse-engineered Dreamina wrappers on shared pooled accounts, Seedance 1.5 misbranded as 2.0 (tell: no native audio, 10s cap instead of 15s), and pure fraud with no model behind it.

Correction: the Google-ban argument does not apply here

My first version of this page said farming risked your client Google Ads — RingOnDemand, LendPeak, the GCALIT MCC — because a multi-account ban cascades by device, IP and payment fingerprint. That reasoning was imported from the Veo/Flow work and does not belong here. BytePlus is ByteDance. Farming BytePlus accounts cannot touch a Google account. Scratch it.

What actually blocks farming on BytePlus is narrower and better evidenced:

  • Identity verification, not just email + phone. The free quota is only available to "identity-verified personal accounts and enterprise accounts". That is a document check, which antidetect browsers and burner numbers do not defeat.
  • A same-identity clause, in writing. BytePlus: "If multiple accounts are associated with the same mobile phone number, the same identification document, the same account ID, or are otherwise determined by BytePlus to be controlled by the same individual or entity, they will be regarded as the same user."
  • Bulk abuse is named as a disqualifier in the same clause — "resource hoarding, bulk abuse" — with the right to disqualify reserved.
  • Sub-accounts don't multiply it. The free quota is explicitly "shared under the primary account", with worked arithmetic in the docs to make the point.

So the objection isn't a catastrophic tail-risk any more. It's simpler: you'd be forging identity documents for $9.44 a go.

And when farming does work, it kills the thing

Midjourney is the precedent. Its CEO, on the record: "We stopped trials because of massive amounts of people making throwaway accounts to get free images." The free tier didn't get cheaper. It got removed, permanently, for everyone.

The free trial you're seeing is real — and it isn't farming

Bob flagged that rival batch tools advertise a trial worth 5–10 videos. They do. But look at whose credits are being spent, because it changes what you should copy.

There are two different free tiers being conflated, and only one of them needs a BytePlus account at all:

  • The official BytePlus grant — 500k tokens per model, one time, behind identity verification. 24 clips at 720p. This is the one that can't be farmed.
  • Reseller signup credits — this is almost certainly what you're looking at. fal.ai gives new accounts $10, no credit card; WaveSpeed and Replicate give smaller signup grants on the same terms; Higgsfield periodically runs unlimited-for-24-hours promos. Email address only. No ID check.

What those sellers are actually doing

They are not farming BytePlus. They are running an ordinary SaaS funnel: the "5–10 free videos" is their own paid credit, given away as a lead magnet and priced into the subscription they want to sell you. fal.ai charges exactly 2× the official token rate — a $10 giveaway costs them $5 of real inference, which is cheap customer acquisition for a recurring plan.

So the thing worth copying from them is the funnel, not the credits.

The practical consequence for us

You do not need a BytePlus account to start. A single legitimate fal.ai signup is ~6 clips of real Seedance 2.0 at 720p, free, today, with no card and no ID. That is enough to judge whether the model is worth building on — which is the only question open right now.

The tool's pool.py already has a provider field for exactly this; the fal.ai adapter is designed but not yet implemented. It is roughly an hour of work and it would let the batch runner spend free reseller credit instead of sitting idle waiting for a BytePlus key.

What has not changed

None of this scales. Free credit is a way to evaluate the model for nothing, not a way to produce at volume. At 24 clips per verified account and ~6 per reseller signup, a hundred videos a week is a paid line item no matter how you route it — roughly $76/week at 720p on the flagship, or $38 on mini. Budget for that, or self-host on the rig.

00:38 · shipped

What I built instead

A standalone batch tool that does the part you actually wanted — hundreds of images and videos without babysitting a browser tab — using keys you legitimately hold. One key drives both video (Seedance 2.0) and images (Seedream 5.0).

# set up once
seedance init
seedance check          # verifies the key, lists models + your concurrency

# then point it at a spreadsheet and walk away
seedance batch jobs.csv

Resume is free

Every state change lands in a JSONL ledger. Kill it, close the laptop, lose the wifi — re-run the same command and it skips what finished and retries only what didn't.

It refuses bad rows before they cost money

Model limits are enforced client-side. A row asking 1080p from the fast model is rejected for free, printed, and counted — never silently dropped.

Clips longer than 15 seconds

Seedance can return the final frame of a clip. seedance chain feeds it into the next one as the opening frame, so five prompts become a continuous 50-second sequence instead of five hard cuts.

Concurrency is the real ceiling

An individual key allows 180 requests a minute but only 3 jobs at once. The runner keeps all three slots full, spreads across multiple keys if you hold them, and reroutes when one dies.

Cost is always read back from the API's own usage figures. Nothing in the tool estimates a price. 18 end-to-end tests pass against a mock server that reproduces throttling, quota exhaustion and revoked keys — no API key needed to run them.
